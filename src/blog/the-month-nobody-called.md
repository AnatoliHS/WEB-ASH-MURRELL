There was a month, early in my commercial photography business, when nobody called.

No emails. No "hey, are you free next week?" Nothing.

And I remember sitting there thinking: do people even know I exist anymore?

## The Part Nobody Explains

When I started out, I went at it with everything I had. I took my portfolio around town, sat down with anyone who'd give me twenty minutes, and lined people up. It worked. I was shooting constantly and meeting a lot of great people.

Then the work took over.

Here's the trap. You need to do the work to earn referrals. But when you're doing the work, you don't have time to go out and earn more of them. So the people who were sending you jobs slowly forget to think of you.

I didn't see it at the time. I just felt the quiet.

## Feast or Famine Is a System Problem

When I was busy, I had no time to talk to people.

When I wasn't busy, I had nothing but time to talk to people.

So I'd get out there, have coffee, reconnect, and within a few weeks it was "oh yeah, how's it going? Would you be up for this?" The work came back. I got busy. I stopped talking to people. And round we went again.

I thought it was luck. It wasn't. The two things were connected, and I didn't understand that yet.

Referrals aren't a strategy. They're a symptom of good work. And symptoms fade if nobody's feeding them.

## It Has to Run Alongside Everything Else

What changed was building a team. Once I could send people out to handle shoots and other parts of the business, I could finally see it clearly: staying in front of people isn't something you do when things go quiet. It has to happen all the time, every week, right alongside the work.

That's the honest part. There are so many layers to running a business, and this one is easy to drop because it never feels urgent until it's too late.

## The Quadrant I Wish I'd Had

Going through those quiet months forced me to look at every way a business can find work. Eventually I started sorting them into four boxes, using two questions: is it expensive or cheap, and is it fast or slow?

**Expensive and slow:** billboards, radio, direct mail, sponsorships, TV. Most legacy media lives here.

**Expensive and fast:** Google and YouTube ads, Meta ads, retargeting, streaming ads. You pay to skip the line.

**Cheap and slow:** SEO, blogs (hi), a YouTube channel, organic content, your Google Business Profile. It compounds, but it takes time.

**Cheap and fast:** texting past customers. Asking for referrals. Following up on old quotes. Calling new leads back in under five minutes.

That last box is where referrals live. It's the fastest, cheapest growth most small businesses have, and it's the one we neglect the moment we get busy.
